Example: WT8 — Paraphrase and Quotation Workshop

REPLACE ME WITH A ONE-LINE INTRODUCTION TO THIS EXAMPLE.

Chatbot

WT8 — Paraphrase and Quotation Workshop v4.2

Paste the original source extract and your attempt to use it. I will check whether your paraphrase or quotation is safe, accurate and properly credited.

User

I have this source extract and my attempt to use it. Please check whether this is safe paraphrase or quotation use.

Source extract: “The city introduced a late-night bus service to reduce pressure on taxis after midnight and improve safety for students travelling home.”

My attempt: The city brought in a late-night bus service to lessen pressure on taxis after midnight and improve safety for students going home (Jones, 2022).

Chatbot

Verdict: this is too close to count as a safe paraphrase.

Lining the two up shows the problem:

SourceYour attempt
introduced a late-night bus servicebrought in a late-night bus service
to reduce pressure on taxis after midnightto lessen pressure on taxis after midnight
improve safety for students travelling homeimprove safety for students going home

You have swapped a few words (introduced → brought in, reduce → lessen, travelling → going) but kept the same sentence structure and order of ideas. This is known as patchwriting, and it is treated as a plagiarism risk even with a citation — not just a style issue. A reader comparing the two would see the source sentence wearing a light disguise.

The citation (Jones, 2022) is needed and correctly placed, but a citation does not make patchwriting safe. You either need a genuine paraphrase in your own words, or an honest direct quotation in quotation marks.

Your task: Put the source out of sight. In one sentence, tell a friend why the city did this and who it was for — then write that down. Paste your new version here and I will check whether it is far enough from the original. I won't write it for you, because the wording needs to be yours.

Move practised: turning a too-close paraphrase into genuinely independent wording.
