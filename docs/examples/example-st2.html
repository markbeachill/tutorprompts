Example: ST2 — Whole-Work Structure Review

REPLACE ME WITH A ONE-LINE INTRODUCTION TO THIS EXAMPLE.

Chatbot

ST2 — Whole-Work Structure Review v4.2

Paste the sections of your piece (headings and a sentence or two each is fine). I will map what each part is doing, point out anything out of order, and then ask you to suggest a better sequence.

User

Introduction: This essay is about social media advertising.

Conclusion: BTS and YouTubers are important.

Methodology: I will use discourse analysis and netnography.

Rationale: This matters because young people use social media and brands want engagement.

Chatbot

Structure map (a reverse outline you could make yourself)

Part as givenWhat it is actually doing
IntroductionNames the topic, but not the question, argument or scope.
ConclusionAsserts importance, but no finding is stated to conclude from.
MethodologyNames two methods, but not what they are applied to.
RationaleExplains why the topic matters — useful, but currently sits late.

What stands out

  • The order is unusual: the conclusion appears second, before the method that would produce it.
  • The rationale (why it matters) usually supports the introduction, so having it last leaves the introduction thin.
  • There is a gap: no research question or aim links the topic to the method.

Over to you

Using the map, how would you reorder these four parts so a reader meets general framing before specific claims, and a claim before its complications? Write the order you think works and I will respond to it. I would rather you propose the sequence than hand you mine straight away.

User

I'm stuck. Just show me an order.

Chatbot

That's fine — here is a sequence that follows general-before-specific and claim-before-complication. The Purpose column is the part to read, because it explains why each section sits where it does.

OrderSectionPurpose (why it belongs here)
1IntroductionSets the general frame so the reader knows the territory before any detail.
2RationaleJustifies the topic right after it is introduced, so the reader accepts why it is worth studying.
3MethodologyComes once the reader knows what is being studied and why; method answers “how”.
4ConclusionComes last because a conclusion should follow from findings, not precede the method that produces them.

I have not written the text of any section — the wording stays yours. One thing still missing from this order is a research question or aim between the rationale and the methodology; you may want to add one. Want to try drafting that link?
