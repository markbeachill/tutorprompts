Example: WT10 — Learn Subjects: Parsing Your Own Sentences

REPLACE ME WITH A ONE-LINE INTRODUCTION TO THIS EXAMPLE.

Chatbot

WT10 — Learn Subjects: Parsing Your Own Sentences v4.2

Paste a few of your own sentences. I will teach you a reliable method for finding the verb and the grammatical subject, one step at a time, and ask you to try it yourself.

User

I always get confused about subjects and verbs. Can you teach me using these sentences?

The council introduced a night bus service. The service was welcomed by students. Because the taxi queue was unsafe after midnight.

Chatbot

Good sentences to learn on. We will use one fixed method every time:

  1. Find the verb first — the word that shows an action or a state of being.
  2. Then ask: who or what is doing or being it? That answer is the grammatical subject.

One thing to fix in your mind from the start: the grammatical subject is not the topic or the main point of the sentence. It is simply the doer of the verb.

Let's do the first sentence together as a model:

The council introduced a night bus service.

Verb? introduced. Now ask: who or what introduced? The council. So the subject is the council.

Your turn. Take sentence 2 — “The service was welcomed by students.” Find the verb first, then tell me who or what is doing or being it. Just answer for that one sentence and I will check it before we go on.

User

I think the subject of the second sentence is students because the sentence is about students.

Chatbot

That is exactly the trap the method is designed to catch, so this is a useful moment.

You picked students because the sentence feels like it is about students. But remember step 1: find the verb first. The verb here is was welcomed. Now step 2: who or what was welcomed? The service. So the grammatical subject is the service, even though students are clearly important to the meaning.

This sentence is passive: the subject (the service) is having something done to it, and the real actor (students) appears after “by”. Grammatical subject and actor are not always the same thing — spotting that difference is a big step.

Now look at sentence 3: “Because the taxi queue was unsafe after midnight.” Try the method on it — but also notice how it feels when you read it on its own. Does it sound finished? Tell me your subject and verb, and whether you think it is a complete sentence.

Once finding subjects feels steadier, WT9 (Flow) or WT2 (Clarity) are good next steps for using this skill on whole paragraphs.
