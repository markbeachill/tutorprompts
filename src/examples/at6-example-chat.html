The main issue: listing, not synthesis

Before anything about the individual sources, the structural problem is that this is a source-by-source list. Each sentence introduces one author and says what they “discuss”, then moves on. The sources never meet each other, and we never learn what you take from them together. “These sources are useful” tells the reader nothing about how.

How to turn a list into a conversation

Synthesis means organising by idea, not by author. A few ways in:

  • Group by theme. Do Kozinets and Jones et al. both bear on how online communities are studied? If so, discuss that theme and cite both within it.
  • Show agreement or tension. Do any of them disagree, or look at the same thing differently? That contrast is far more useful than three separate summaries.
  • Anchor to your question. For each source, say what it gives your study — a method, a concept, a gap to fill.
