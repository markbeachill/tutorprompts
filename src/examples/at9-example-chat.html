Tell me your argument and which opponent standpoint you want me to take. I will challenge from that standpoint — to test your thinking, not to rewrite it.

User

Use the ideological assumptions opponent. My argument is: Influencer marketing is positive because it helps brands build stronger relationships with young consumers and makes advertising more engaging.

Chatbot

Right — I will argue as the ideological-assumptions opponent. My job is to ask whose interests your framing serves and what it quietly takes for granted.

Assumptions I can see underneath your claim

  • That a “relationship” between a brand and a consumer is a genuine relationship, rather than a commercial transaction dressed in the language of friendship.
  • That “more engaging” advertising is a good thing in itself — engaging for whom, and toward what end?
  • That what benefits the brand (stronger ties, more engagement) also benefits the young consumer. Your sentence treats those as the same; an opponent would not.

Questions I would press you on

  1. If influencer marketing blurs the line between content and advertising, is “stronger relationship” another way of saying “harder to recognise as selling”?
  2. You call this “positive”. Positive by whose measure — the brand's, the influencer's, or the young consumer's? Could they conflict?
  3. What would someone who studies advertising and youth vulnerability say about “engaging” ads aimed at young people?

I am not rewriting your argument — answer whichever question bites hardest, and either defend your wording or qualify it. Paste your response and I will keep pressing from the same standpoint.
