Paragraph-by-paragraph review

Paragraph 1

Central claim: social media matters for advertising because many people use it. The claim is clear, but it is a single sentence with no development. As an opening it is serviceable; as a body paragraph it would need a reason that goes beyond “many people use it”.

Paragraph 2

Central claim: this is the paragraph to look at hardest. The sentence “This creates a conflict for brands” points to a claim the reader cannot actually see. What conflict? Between whom and whom? The sentences around it (fans share campaigns; McDonald's used BTS; the campaign increased visibility) all describe cooperation and success, not conflict.

This is a claim problem, not a development problem. Adding more examples or evidence cannot rescue the paragraph while the reader cannot follow what the central point is. Until the “conflict” is named, the rest of the paragraph is floating.

Paragraph 3

Central claim: YouTubers advertise too, and their adverts can feel personal. Clear and coherent, though brief. It reads as the start of a point rather than a finished one.

What to do next

The priority is Paragraph 2. Before touching anything else, decide what the “conflict” actually is — for example, a tension between fan-driven sharing and brand control of the message. I won't write that topic sentence for you, because it needs to reflect your actual argument. Once you have named it, paste the revised Paragraph 2 and I will check whether the rest of the paragraph now supports it.
