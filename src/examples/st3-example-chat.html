Where the reasoning overreaches

Three words are doing more work than the evidence can support:

  • “prove” — a single set of campaigns can illustrate or suggest, but rarely proves. “Prove” promises certainty you would struggle to defend.
  • “no longer respond” — this is an absolute claim. Even if BTS campaigns did well, that does not show consumers have stopped responding to traditional advertising entirely.
  • “all brands should” — a universal recommendation. What worked for one global music act may not transfer to, say, a local insurance firm.

The logic jump

There is also a cause-effect leap: from “these campaigns succeeded” to “therefore celebrities should replace ordinary adverts”. Success in one case does not establish that the celebrity was the cause, or that the alternative is worse. The argument would be stronger as a careful claim: some celebrity-led campaigns can outperform conventional adverts for particular audiences.

What I can and cannot judge

I can see the internal logic problems above from the text alone. What I cannot confirm is the factual side — whether BTS campaigns actually outperformed traditional advertising, and by what measure. That is a subject-knowledge and evidence question; check it against your sources or a tutor in your field rather than taking my word for it.
